# Design: LIMITLINE MVP — personal-limit challenges for age-group triathletes

## Problem Statement
Triathletes get rewarded for **volume**, not **effort**. Strava's sponsored challenges, and rewards apps like Edge, Muuvr and the bundled discounts in TEMPR or CREDOTri, all pay out for distance, streaks or workouts logged. A pro's easy week and a beginner's hardest week earn the same badge, so the reward means nothing. That's the gap behind the founder's pitch that LIMITLINE is "there to test the human limits and it also rewards you for that."

LIMITLINE sets each athlete a challenge calibrated to **their own** recent data, checks the attempt automatically from their workout file, and ranks people by how far they went **beyond their own limit**.

None yet. This is honest and normal for an idea-stage project.

## Status Quo
What age-group triathletes use today, and what's wrong with it:
- **Strava challenges:** volume-based (distance, duration, streak, days active). Brands paid about $20k per sponsored challenge as of 2024 ([Modern Retail](https://www.modernretail.co/marketing/brands-like-chipotle-duer-hoka-are-partnering-with-strava-to-make-branded-workouts/), [Sacra](https://sacra.com/research/strava)). Everyone gets the same badge.
- **Discount perks:** TEMPR and CREDOTri already bundle partner discounts on gear, nutrition and race entries. Discounts are **not** a differentiator.
- **Earn-by-activity:** Muuvr earns Challenge Family race entries from logged activity. Edge Rewards (announced to launch Nov 2026, per its own site: [findyouredge.app](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/strava-challenges-vs-edge-rewards-uk-2026)) gives points per workout.
- **Personalized plans:** Stamina personalizes *training plans*, but has no challenges or rewards.
- **Known failure mode:** fitness and reward apps lose most users within 30 days, and rewards for one-off activity stop feeling special. These figures are approximate: they come mostly from vendor blogs ([Sahha](https://sahha.ai/blog/health-app-churn-retention/), [Edge](https://www.findyouredge.app/news/why-most-fitness-reward-apps-disappoint-uk-2026)) and trade press ([ukactive](https://www.ukactive.com/?p=22482)), not peer-reviewed studies.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge
- **User:** amateur age-group triathletes who train solo. This is a type the founder has observed, not yet a named person. Turning that type into 10 real names is the first assignment.
- **Wedge:** the personal-limit challenge engine. Upload past runs and rides, get a weekly challenge just beyond your own best, upload your attempt, get an automatic pass or fail.
- **Scope cut:** v1 calibrates **run and bike only**. Swim waits, because pool and open-water data from watches is unreliable and would mean a third calibration engine.

## Constraints
- Solo founder, beginner developer, so every choice should favor simple, well-documented tools.
- No brands required for v1 (premise 3). Rewards are small and hand-delivered by the founder.
- Workout data comes from **file uploads** (.fit from Garmin and most watches, .gpx from almost anything). Strava's API starts new third-party apps limited to the developer's own account until Strava approves them, so it isn't usable for a pilot.
- GPS files contain home and work locations. **The LIMITLINE app never receives raw files** (exceptions: the consented pre-pilot collection in The Assignment, and the screenshot evidence in the fallback gate): the athlete's browser (on a phone or a computer) reads each .fit/.gpx file on that device and sends only the numbers we need: activity start time, sport, best efforts, elevation gain per km, average power, and a fingerprint (hash) of the file to detect duplicates. Heart rate isn't stored, because v1 doesn't use it. No GPS tracks or raw files are stored. Founder outlier review uses only those stored numbers. If an attempt looks wrong, the founder asks the athlete directly.
- **Verification is trust-based in v1.** The numbers are computed in the athlete's browser, so a determined athlete could edit them or the file. The checks below *discourage* casual cheating but can't stop deliberate cheating. That's acceptable for a 10-person pilot of people the founder knows by name.
- **Getting files onto the device:** exporting original files is easiest from a computer. Garmin Connect's and Strava's export options are mainly on their websites, and Strava's bulk export arrives as an emailed .zip. So the first history upload will often happen on a computer, with weekly attempts from either device. `/plan-eng-review` should confirm the easiest weekly path for phone users.
- **Supported uploads:** pick several .fit, .gpx or .fit.gz files at once, or a whole unzipped folder, using the browser's built-in pickers. Strava bulk-export .zip upload is deferred (eng review D6).
- **Privacy & visibility:**
  - The Run and Bike boards are visible **only to signed-in pilot members**, never at a public URL.
  - Names show as first name plus last initial ("Priya R."). Any athlete can **hide from the boards** and still get challenges and draw entries.
  - The share card includes the name only if the athlete chooses to.
  - The **admin page is founder-only**, checked on the server and not just hidden in the menu.
  - **Data deletion:** an athlete can ask the founder by email, and the founder deletes their account and all stored numbers within 7 days. It's a manual process in v1; a button can come later.
- GitHub push from this session is currently blocked: the Claude GitHub App isn't installed on `shaaningit/limitline`.

## Premises
1. **The wedge is the personal-limit challenge, not the rewards.** Discounts, coaching, global chat and brand content are deferred until challenges alone keep people coming back.
2. **First users are solo age-group triathletes, calibrating run and bike only** (revised after the second opinion). The brand stays triathlon; swim comes later.
3. **The MVP does not depend on brands.** Early rewards are hand-delivered by the founder. Signing one small brand in parallel is welcome but not required.
4. **Reuse workouts athletes already record** via .fit/.gpx upload rather than building tracking or depending on the Strava API.
5. **Mobile-friendly website first**, not an App Store app. Faster to build, no Apple review, shareable by link.

## Approaches Considered
### Approach A: Concierge test (no app)
The founder calibrates and verifies by hand, using a form, a spreadsheet and WhatsApp. Effort: S (human ~1 day / CC ~1 hour). It's the fastest proof of demand but doesn't scale past ~15 athletes. Kept as the fallback if building stalls.
### Approach B: Upload → Calibrate → Verify web app + share card (chosen)
Effort: M (human ~2-3 weeks / CC ~1-2 days). Builds the defensible part directly and is the foundation for rewards, brands and swim later.
### Approach C: Limit Test + shareable Limit Card
One standard all-out test, a 4-week block to beat it, then a retest card. Effort: S-M. Not chosen because the 4-week loop gives slower feedback. Its **share card** is borrowed into B.

## Recommended Approach
**Approach B with C's share card.** The user said "no preference" and accepted the recommendation by default. It can be changed at approval.

Core loop (wireframe: `docs/designs/limitline-mvp-sketch.png` (source: `docs/designs/limitline-mvp-sketch.html`)):
1. **Sign up** with an email magic link, a one-click login link with no password to forget. Set your time zone.
2. **Upload 6-8 weeks** of run and bike files. They're read in your browser on your own device (see Constraints), and the app finds your limit for each v1 metric (below).
3. **Weekly challenge:** your limit plus a small step (starting at about +2-3%), with a "why this number" line so the bar feels fair.
4. **Upload the attempt.** The browser measures it and sends only the numbers; the **server** decides pass or fail with the margin shown (eng review D9).
5. **Share card** on a pass ("+3.4% beyond my limit"), sized for Instagram stories.
6. **Two Limit boards, Run and Bike,** each ranked by % beyond each athlete's own limit, so a beginner can top them.
7. **Admin page** listing each week's passers, with a reward draw and a "mark as given" button.

### v1 rules (what makes it fair)
These settle what the first draft left open. `/plan-eng-review` may refine them.

**Metrics.** v1 uses only measurements that can be checked fairly from the file:
- **Outdoor, GPS-recorded only.** Treadmill, virtual-run and indoor smart-trainer files are rejected, with the reason shown. Their distance or power comes from machine estimates and isn't comparable to outdoor efforts.
- **Run:** fastest continuous 5 km or 10 km. Best efforts inside a longer run count, e.g. the fastest 5 km within a 12 km run. The effort counts only if that stretch is roughly flat, with elevation gain of 15 m/km or less. Hillier efforts are ignored, not failed.
- **Bike:** best 20-minute average power, **only for files with power-meter data.** Bike speed depends too much on wind and hills, so riders without a power meter get run challenges only in v1.
- No heart-rate-based calibration in v1. That's Open Question 2's adaptive version, for later.

**% beyond limit, per metric** (always "higher = better"):
- Run: `(limit time − attempt time) ÷ limit time × 100`
- Bike: `(attempt watts − limit watts) ÷ limit watts × 100`

Run and bike percentages aren't comparable (power climbs faster than pace for the same fitness gain), so **runs and rides are ranked on separate boards.** The wireframe's single mixed board is superseded.

**The limit.** Your limit for each metric is your best qualifying effort in the uploaded history, from the 8 weeks before sign-up. After that, **any** later valid activity that beats it raises it, whether a challenge attempt, a normal workout or a race. The limit never goes down in v1.

**Each challenge freezes its limit and target when issued.** Pass or fail and % beyond limit are always measured against that frozen limit. Any raise from this week's uploads applies starting next week's challenge. Every upload during the challenge week that matches the challenge's metric counts as an attempt. A non-attempt activity can raise the limit only if it was recorded after sign-up, passes the duplicate check, and meets the metric rules.

**Minimum history.** A metric gets challenges only once the history contains at least 4 qualifying efforts for it. With fewer, the athlete is told to upload more or wait for more workouts.

**What counts as a valid attempt.** All of these must hold, or the upload is rejected with the reason shown:
- The start time **recorded inside the file** falls in the challenge week: Monday 00:00 to Sunday 23:59 in the athlete's own time zone, after the challenge was issued. Checking the file's own timestamp discourages uploading an old best run that was held back.
- The sport and metric match the challenge.
- Its file fingerprint and start time don't match anything already uploaded.
- It meets the metric rule above (a flat enough 5 or 10 km for runs, power data for bikes).

**Who gets rewarded.** Every athlete who passes that week's challenge earns one entry. Each week the founder draws **one winner at random from all passers** for the hand-delivered reward. It's a random draw rather than the board's top spot, so a beginner's pass is worth exactly as much as a pro's, and topping the board wins nothing extra. If a brand donates codes, every passer gets one until they run out. The admin page lists the week's passers, runs the draw, and records the reward as given.

**Known v1 risk: sandbagging.** An athlete can leave their best efforts out of the history to get an easier target. The minimum-history rule limits this. The founder also reviews any pass more than twice the step (e.g. over 6% beyond limit), or any first-week pass, before a reward or code is given.

**Fallback gate.** If the in-browser reader can't reliably pull these metrics from the 10 pilot athletes' real files, the pilot runs as **Approach A:** the founder sets challenges from the athletes' pre-pilot files, and each athlete proves an attempt by sending a **screenshot of the activity summary** from their watch app or Strava (date, distance, time, elevation gain or average power) to their private folder. The founder confirms pass or fail from it. Screenshots follow the same deletion deadline as pre-pilot files.

Explicitly **not** in v1: swim, Strava API, native app, brand portal, discounts marketplace, coaching, chat, payments, automated anti-cheat.

Proposed tools (to be locked in `/plan-eng-review`, not decided here):
- **Next.js**, a popular framework for building websites with React.
- **Supabase**, a hosted database with login, so there's no server to run. No file storage is used, because raw files stay on the athlete's device.
- A **transactional email service** (e.g. Resend, connected to Supabase as custom SMTP) to send the login links. Supabase's built-in email is rate-limited and meant only for testing.
- **Vercel** for hosting, with free automatic deploys from GitHub.
- An open-source **FIT/GPX parser** library.

## Open Questions
1. **Is the flat-run rule right?** v1 settles fairness with flat 5 or 10 km runs and power-only bike challenges (see v1 rules). Still open: whether 15 m/km is the right flatness cut-off, how noisy GPS affects 5 km times, and when bike riders without power meters get challenges (repeat segments are a candidate). Test it on the 10 pilot athletes' files. The fallback gate covers failure.
2. **How big is each step?** Start at a fixed +2-3%, or adapt to how fast the athlete has been improving?
3. **What about athletes with little history?** One option is a single baseline test effort (borrowed from Approach C).
4. **How do we handle cheating** (e-bikes, car segments, edited files)? v1 relies on trust plus the founder reviewing outliers by hand.
5. **Will a small brand join?** The founder plans to approach small brands. It's uncertain, and the plan works either way.
7. **Embers numbers (outside voice #11):** base embers per quest, shop prices, item stock and a prize budget cap. The founder sets these before Nov 16; amounts are config values.
6. **Settled in eng review (D9-D16).** Original item: weekly challenge rules, deferred to `/plan-eng-review` by the founder's choice:
   - how many challenges each athlete gets per week
   - who picks run vs bike, and what "Swap" does
   - the retry policy, and which attempt counts for the board, share card and draw
   - when challenges are issued, including for mid-week sign-ups, and whether the time zone can change
   - the board period (this week's best attempt vs all-time), and whether 5 km and 10 km share the Run board

   The wireframe's "why this number" text, which mentions a run trend and heart rate, is illustrative only. v1 explains the fixed step from your limit.

## Success Criteria
**Eng review D20 (supersedes the challenge-based bullets below):** an athlete "came back" if they submitted at least one valid activity in BOTH week 3 and week 4. Success = 5+ of 10 came back; rethink = under 4; exactly 4 = run 2 more weeks. Also track, per athlete, embers from completing quests vs from beating their limit, and ask every non-returner one question.

A 4-week pilot:
- **10 named** solo age-group triathletes upload their history (a list of names, not a sign-up count).
- **7 or more** attempt challenge 1.
- **5 or more** come back and attempt challenge 2. This is the real test: does the challenge itself bring people back?
- **3 or more** share a result card without being asked.
- **Stretch:** 1 small brand donates rewards for the pilot.
- **Kill signal:** if fewer than 4 of 10 attempt a second challenge, the personal-limit challenge doesn't retain users, and we revisit the wedge before building more.
- **Gray zone (exactly 4 of 10):** run 2 more weeks with a smaller step (+1-2%), then decide using the same thresholds.
- **Telling "too hard" apart from "not wanted":** for each athlete, record challenge 1's pass or fail and margin next to whether they came back. Ask every non-returner one question: *"Was it too hard, too easy, too much hassle to upload, or just not for you?"* If most say "too hard" or "hassle," that's a calibration or usability fix, not proof the idea failed.

## Why the pilot uses the app, not just concierge
The pilot tests two things: **do personal-limit challenges bring people back** (demand), and **can the limit be set and checked automatically and fairly** (the engine). Concierge mode tests only the first. Building the app costs no extra calendar time, because it happens while recruiting is still underway (see Timeline). An instant automatic result is also part of what we're testing. Concierge stays as the fallback.
