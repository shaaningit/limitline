# Launch campaign: reels

## The promise

**Beat your limit. Get rewarded.**

Every athlete-facing ad sells this one promise and ends on it. Strava, Garmin and Nike already
show your personal bests for free, so "beat your limit" alone is not a reason to download us.
What only LIMITLINE does is what happens *after* you beat it:

1. **You get rewarded.** Beating your limit earns embers, and embers buy real gear in the shop.
2. **Brands back you.** Brands sponsor quests and you win their gear, the way pros get sponsored.
3. **The board is fair.** It ranks % beyond your *own* limit, so a beginner can beat a pro.

"Cross your limit" stays as the brand feel (the logo is a line being broken), but the ads lead
with the reward.

### How the three arcs fit

- **Product arc** (quests, rewards, fair board): the promise itself. It is in every athlete ad.
- **Sponsorship arc**: athletes only see it as "win gear from brands" (reel 05). The business pitch
  (reel 04) goes to brands through email and LinkedIn, never in the athlete feed.
- **Content arc**: athletes' own posts ("I did this quest and won this") are the proof. Repost
  them. They are the cheapest and most believable ads we will have.

From campaign to campaign, change the **hook**, not the promise.

## Before posting

- **Link in bio** must open the app's sign-up page, and the app must be live.
- **Rewards must be real.** The reward ads only work if the shop has real items people can redeem.
  Stock the shop before launch, even if you fund the first items yourself.
- **"Get sponsored" is a hook, not a contract.** Reel 05 says right away what it means: brands
  sponsor quests and you win their gear. Keep that line in any edit, and don't promise
  sponsorship deals.
- **Sample brands**: MOXA TRAIL and HALDO are the app's sample sponsors, not real companies.
  Reels 05 and 06 say "Illustrative example" on screen. Swap in real partners (with their
  permission) as soon as they sign, and re-export.
- **Made-up athletes**: Maya, Leo and the riders in reels 02 and 04 are examples, and those
  reels say so on screen.
- **Embers and prices**: +20 embers for beating your limit follows the app's current rule (10 for
  a quest, doubled for a beat). The base amount is still a placeholder in `lib/rules/config.ts`.
  If you change it, re-export reels 05 and 06 to match.
- **Strava**: several reels say results are "checked with" or "synced from" Strava. Strava has
  brand rules for apps that use its data. Read them before running *paid* ads, and change the
  wording if needed.
- **Heart fact** (reel 01): "up to 20 litres a minute" is the peak figure usually quoted for
  well-trained athletes. Keep the "up to".

## What to measure after 48 hours

- **3-second hold**: the share of viewers still watching after 3 s. This tells you if the hook works.
- **Average watch time / completion**: tells you if the story holds to the promise at the end.
- **Shares and saves**: tells you if people want to send it to a training partner.
- **Profile visits and link clicks**: tells you if it turns into sign-ups. This is the one that counts.

Make more ads in the style that wins on link clicks. A cheap variation is to keep the reel and
change only the hook, or swap the sport (a swim or hike version of 05 or 06).
